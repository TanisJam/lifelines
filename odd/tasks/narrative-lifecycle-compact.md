### CP1-B design (fixed decisions)

Goal: keep the ENCODE peak inside the already-measured 4-year envelope (~1 GB) while persisting a
life of any length, without weakening one byte of authority.

The existing `lifecycle-capsule-compact-cb1-1` format is CERTIFIED and stays untouched. CP1-B adds a
NEW, separately versioned chunked format; a stored life declares which schema it uses and each loader
refuses the other. No implicit migration in either direction.

1. **Chunk.** A chunk covers a contiguous year range `[from..to]` of one life and carries exactly that
   range's journal records and nothing else. It does NOT carry the accumulated world state: `load`
   regenerates that from genesis, so storing it per chunk would make every chunk grow with the whole
   life and defeat the purpose. A chunk is encoded with the same compact DAG codec and wire profile,
   so its encode cost depends only on its own years. The chunk size is chosen so one chunk's encode
   peak stays inside the measured 4-year envelope (starting default: 4 journal years, configurable
   downward, never raised without a measurement).
2. **Manifest.** One immutable, exact-key-set manifest per `(lifeId, branchId)`:
   `version`, `lifeId`, `branchId`, `genesisDigest`, `capsuleSchema` (the chunked one), `chunkYears`,
   `frontier`, `status`, and an ordered `chunks` list of `{ from, to, digest }`. Immutability triggers
   mirror the existing lifecycle binding table. The manifest is NEVER authority: it is an index that
   must be corroborated by regeneration.
3. **Save.** One full current-authority regeneration produces the complete output ONCE; the journal is
   then sliced into chunks and each chunk is encoded sequentially from that same in-memory output (so
   the peak is one chunk's encode plus the runtime state, not N replays). All chunks, the manifest and
   the binding are written in ONE transaction. A digest is always recomputed from the admitted chunk.
4. **Load.** Read the binding, require its recognized version and the chunked schema, then regenerate
   the WHOLE life from genesis (full replay, as today), and validate every chunk against the
   regenerated journal range. A missing chunk, a foreign schema, a digest mismatch or a range
   disagreement is a REFUSAL, never a repair, never a partial view. The artifact returned to callers is
   the REGENERATED capsule, never a reassembly of stored bytes; the stored chunks exist to corroborate
   that regeneration, not to supply it.
5. **No second authority.** The chunk chain cannot stand in for replay: nothing may be read from a
   chunk without a successful regeneration of that chunk's range. A chunk chain whose links are all
   self-consistent but disagree with regeneration is still refused.
6. **Boundary.** The old compact path keeps working unchanged (existing lives, existing tests). The
   chunked path is additive and selected by schema.

Open item carried, not solved by B: the runtime's own peak (3.9 GB at 46 years) is driven by the
journal retaining two full state copies per year; B bounds the ENCODE peak, not the run peak. A
60-year life will still need C (or an equivalent runtime memory reduction) before it can run at all.

### B4 result — CP1-B alone is NOT sufficient (measured conclusively)
Three end-to-end measurements of a 20-year life (`cap-0`, 136 people at the end) all failed at chunk
creation with `Invalid or oversized lifecycle data dictionary`, with chunk widths of 4, 2 and 1 year.
With 1-year chunks there is nothing left to split: the ATOM is a single year's journal record, and a
late record on its own already exceeds the 32,000,000 work-text ceiling. The cause is the record
itself, not the partitioning: `LifecycleYearRecord` stores `postNative` (the full world state) plus
`final: structuredClone(draft)` (a SECOND full copy), plus the physical snapshot and the economics, so
one late record is roughly 2.5-4 MB of wire and 30M+ work-text units at ~136 people.

Conclusion: chunking bounds how much is encoded at once only down to one record, and one record is
already too big for a long life. CP1-B therefore REQUIRES the per-record reduction (option C) before
it can serve a product-meaningful lifespan; C is a prerequisite, not a companion. The certified
4-year fixture passes only because its records are ~6.7M each at 85 people.

The design section's fixed-year chunking is also superseded: the chunk width must be size-driven, and
after C the correct starting point is 1-2 years per chunk at the populations measured here.

`0`, and `admitLifecycleGenesis` re-digests a canonical form that distinguishes `-0` and own
`undefined`, so EVERY real created life was written but unreadable (`Lifecycle digest
disagreement`). B2's own fixture was a 6-founder / 11-person genesis with no signed zeros, so its
